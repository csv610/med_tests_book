\chapter{Metanephrines}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Metanephrine and normetanephrine testing measures metabolites of epinephrine and norepinephrine. Plasma free metanephrines or 24-hour urinary fractionated metanephrines are the preferred biochemical tests for suspected pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma.

\section*{Alternative Names}
Plasma Free Metanephrines; Fractionated Urine Metanephrines; Metanephrine and Normetanephrine.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
Testing is used for episodic or resistant hypertension, characteristic spells of headache, sweating, and palpitations, an adrenal mass with concerning features, hereditary-risk syndromes, or previous pheochromocytoma or paraganglioma. It is not recommended as indiscriminate screening of asymptomatic low-risk patients.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
For plasma testing, blood is usually collected after the patient has rested quietly, preferably in a supine position. Urine testing measures fractionated metanephrines over a complete 24-hour collection. Markedly elevated results require specialist evaluation and appropriate localization testing.

\section*{Preparation, Risks, and Limitations}
Follow instructions regarding caffeine, nicotine, alcohol, strenuous exercise, and medicines that may interfere. Stress, illness, posture, and incomplete urine collection can cause false-positive results. Blood collection has minimal risk.

\section*{Understanding Results}
Small elevations are often caused by collection conditions, medicines, or illness and commonly require repeat testing under controlled conditions. Large, persistent elevations are more concerning for catecholamine-secreting tumors. Results must be interpreted with symptoms, family history, and imaging ordered only when biochemical evidence or clinical suspicion supports it.

\section*{References}
Endocrine Society clinical practice guidance on pheochromocytoma and paraganglioma.

\section*{Regulatory Status and Authorization Date}
Regulatory status applies to the specific assay, instrument, manufacturer, and intended use rather than to the test name alone. No single approval date can be assigned to this test category. For a commercial assay, verify the current product in the relevant regulator's database: the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) clearance, approval, or authorization; India's Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) licence or permission; the European Union In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation (EU IVDR) CE certificate issued through the applicable conformity-assessment route; or the United Kingdom Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) registration and UKCA/CE status. Laboratory-developed tests may not have an individual FDA, CDSCO, EU IVDR, or MHRA approval date.
