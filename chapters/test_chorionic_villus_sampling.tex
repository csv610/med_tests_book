\chapter{Chorionic Villus Sampling (CVS)}
\section*{What Is This Test?}
Chorionic villus sampling is a prenatal diagnostic procedure that removes a small sample of placental tissue for genetic and chromosomal testing. It can provide a diagnosis earlier in pregnancy than amniocentesis for many conditions.
\section*{Alternative Names}
\begin{itemize}\item CVS\item Chorionic Villus Biopsy\item Prenatal Diagnostic Placental Sampling\end{itemize}
\section*{Principle}
Cells from chorionic villi share the fetus's genetic material. Testing may include chromosome analysis, chromosomal microarray, or targeted molecular testing. Placental mosaicism can occasionally complicate interpretation.
\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
CVS may be offered after an abnormal screening result or ultrasound finding, or when there is a family or pregnancy history of a genetic condition. It does not generally test for open neural-tube defects; ultrasound and maternal serum screening address those questions.
\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test}
CVS is a diagnostic test, not a screening test. Cell-free DNA and other prenatal screens estimate risk and do not replace diagnostic sampling when certainty is needed.
\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
With ultrasound guidance, a catheter through the cervix or a needle through the abdomen collects placental villi. The route depends on placental position and local expertise. It is usually performed between 10 and 13 weeks.
\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
Follow the maternal-fetal medicine team's instructions. Discuss blood type, Rh status, medicines, bleeding disorders, and prior screening or ultrasound results.
\section*{Contraindications and Precautions}
The procedure is voluntary. Cramping, bleeding, infection, fluid leakage, and a small risk of pregnancy loss can occur. Confirmatory amniocentesis may be needed when placental and fetal findings could differ.
\section*{Understanding Results}
A normal result means that the tested conditions were not detected by the methods used. Abnormal or uncertain results require genetic counseling and specialist interpretation.
\section*{Follow-up Tests}
Follow-up may include maternal serum alpha-fetoprotein, detailed ultrasound, amniocentesis, parental testing, or fetal echocardiography.
