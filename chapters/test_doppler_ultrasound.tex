\chapter{Doppler Ultrasound}
\section*{What Is This Test?} Doppler ultrasound uses sound waves to assess the direction and speed of blood flow through vessels or the heart.
\section*{Alternative Names} Vascular ultrasound; duplex ultrasound; color Doppler.
\section*{Principle} Frequency shifts in reflected sound estimate flow; B-mode imaging shows anatomy while Doppler displays velocity and waveform.
\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered} It evaluates suspected blood clots, narrowed arteries, poor circulation, aneurysm, venous insufficiency, or blood flow in pregnancy and the heart.
\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test} It is a noninvasive primary imaging study for many vascular questions; CT, MRI, or angiography may be secondary studies.
\section*{How This Test Is Performed} Gel and a transducer are moved over the skin; the patient may need position changes or brief breath holding.
\section*{What Preparation Is Needed} Preparation varies by body region; fasting may be required for abdominal studies. Wear loose clothing.
\section*{Understanding Results} The report describes vessel patency, narrowing, blockage, flow direction, velocity, and waveform. Results depend on technique and anatomy.
\section*{Follow-up Tests} CT or MR angiography, venography, echocardiography, or vascular consultation may follow.
