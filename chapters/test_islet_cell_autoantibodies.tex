\chapter{Islet-Cell Autoantibody Test}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
This blood test looks for antibodies that attack the insulin-making cells in the pancreas. These antibodies can support a diagnosis of type 1 diabetes or a slowly developing form of autoimmune diabetes in adults.

\section*{Alternative Names}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Islet Cell Antibodies
  \item ICA
  \item Pancreatic Islet-Cell Autoantibodies
  \item Diabetes Autoantibody Testing
\end{itemize}
\section*{How It Is Performed}
The laboratory tests blood serum. A panel may include GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8, insulin antibodies, and classic islet-cell antibodies. The exact tests depend on the clinical question and the laboratory.

\section*{Why It Is Ordered}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Distinguishing autoimmune type 1 diabetes from type 2 diabetes or other diabetes types when the clinical presentation is uncertain
  \item Evaluating lean-onset diabetes, rapid loss of insulin production, or diabetes with other autoimmune disease
  \item Assessing selected relatives of people with type 1 diabetes in research or specialist risk-stratification programs
\end{itemize}

\section*{Interpretation and Limitations}
A positive result supports autoimmune diabetes but does not show how much insulin the pancreas still makes or when diabetes will begin. A negative result does not completely rule out type 1 diabetes. The clinician also considers glucose, HbA1c, C-peptide, symptoms, age, and how the illness has developed.
\section*{Specimen and Preparation}
Fasting is usually not needed for the antibody test. Glucose, cholesterol, or other tests taken at the same visit may have different instructions. Ask which antibodies are being tested because the tests do not all have the same use.

\section*{Risks and Safety}
The blood draw may cause brief pain or bruising. A positive result does not mean that a person is immediately sick. Excessive thirst, frequent urination, vomiting, abdominal pain, deep breathing, confusion, or severe weakness needs urgent medical care.

\section*{Understanding Results and Follow-up}
The report should name each antibody and say whether it is positive, negative, or unclear. Several positive antibodies usually mean a higher chance of autoimmune diabetes than one weak positive result, but risk is not the same as a diagnosis. Follow-up may include glucose, HbA1c, C-peptide, repeat testing, and diabetes education. Do not change treatment based only on this test.
