\chapter{Carbamazepine Level}
\section*{What Is This Test?}
The carbamazepine level measures the serum concentration of carbamazepine, an iminostilbene anticonvulsant and mood stabilizer used for the treatment of focal (partial) seizures, generalized tonic-clonic seizures, trigeminal neuralgia (first-line, drug of choice), and bipolar disorder (mood stabilizer, second-line) \cite{kwan2011}. Carbamazepine stabilizes neuronal membranes by binding to and prolonging the inactivated state of voltage-gated sodium channels, preventing high-frequency repetitive neuronal firing. Carbamazepine has a narrow therapeutic index (4--12 mcg/mL). Carbamazepine induces its own metabolism by CYP3A4 (autoinduction) over the first 3--6 weeks of therapy, requiring dose increases to maintain therapeutic levels. It is a potent inducer of multiple CYP enzymes (CYP3A4, CYP1A2, CYP2C9, CYP2C19, CYP2B6) and UGT enzymes. Carbamazepine reduces the serum concentrations of oral contraceptives (contraceptive failure), warfarin, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, other anticonvulsants, and many other drugs. Carbamazepine is metabolized by CYP3A4 to carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide, an active metabolite that contributes to neurotoxicity (dizziness, ataxia, diplopia). Toxicity: neurotoxicity (ataxia, nystagmus, diplopia, dizziness, sedation --- the most common dose-limiting side effects); serious adverse effects include agranulocytosis, aplastic anemia, Stevens-Johnson syndrome/toxic epidermal necrolysis (SJS/TEN, strongly associated with HLA-B*15:02 in Asian populations --- genetic screening is recommended before initiating), hyponatremia (SIADH-like syndrome from increased renal sensitivity to ADH, 5--20\% of patients), and teratogenicity (neural tube defects/spina bifida with first-trimester exposure).
\section*{Alternative Names}
Tegretol Level; Carbatrol Level; Carbamazepine Serum Concentration; Carbamazepine-10,11-Epoxide
\section*{Principle}
Carbamazepine is measured by particle-enhanced turbidimetric inhibition immunoassay (PETINIA) or enzyme immunoassay on automated analyzers. The active metabolite carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide is not routinely measured by immunoassays but can be quantified by LC-MS/MS.
\section*{History}
Carbamazepine was synthesized in the late 1950s by the Swiss pharmaceutical company Geigy (now Novartis) during a search for new psychotropic agents; it was initially studied for its tricyclic-like properties. In 1960, Wilhelm Theobald demonstrated its anticonvulsant effect in animal models, and clinical use for epilepsy followed shortly thereafter. It was introduced into clinical practice in Europe in the early 1960s and was approved by the U.S. FDA for trigeminal neuralgia in 1968 and for epilepsy in 1974. Carbamazepine remains a first-line agent for focal seizures and the drug of choice for trigeminal neuralgia.
\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
TDM every 3--6 months during stable therapy. Monitoring during dose changes or interacting drug changes. Diagnosis of carbamazepine toxicity. Pre-screening for HLA-B*15:02 in at-risk Asian populations.
\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test}
This is a secondary, therapeutic-monitoring test rather than a diagnostic test. Carbamazepine levels are used to guide and confirm dosing in patients already taking the drug, to evaluate inadequate response or suspected toxicity, and to detect the pharmacokinetic changes caused by autoinduction or interacting drugs.
\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
Blood is collected by standard venipuncture into a plain serum tube (red-top) or a serum separator tube (SST, gold-top). A trough level is drawn immediately before the next scheduled dose, at steady state (typically 3--5 weeks after starting or changing the dose because of autoinduction). The serum carbamazepine concentration is measured by particle-enhanced turbidimetric inhibition immunoassay (PETINIA) or enzyme immunoassay on automated analyzers. Results are reported in mcg/mL and are typically available within hours.
\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
No fasting or special preparation is required. The level must be drawn as a true trough, immediately before the next dose, and the dosing schedule should be consistent for at least 1--2 weeks; because of autoinduction, steady state is not reached until 3--5 weeks after a dose change. Timing errors are the most common cause of misleading carbamazepine levels \cite{patsalos2008}.
\section*{Contraindications and Precautions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Interpret the result against the time of sampling and the dosing schedule; only a true trough is valid for TDM
  \item Autoinduction lowers carbamazepine levels over the first 3--6 weeks of therapy; repeat the level after dose stabilization
  \item Carbamazepine is a potent inducer of CYP3A4 and other enzymes and lowers levels of oral contraceptives, warfarin, cyclosporine, and many other drugs; recheck levels after any interacting drug change
  \item Carbamazepine-10,11-epoxide, the active metabolite, contributes to neurotoxicity (ataxia, dizziness, diplopia) but is not measured by standard immunoassays; consider LC-MS/MS if epoxide toxicity is suspected
  \item Evaluate for toxicity if nystagmus, ataxia, diplopia, or drowsiness develop, and check the free level if serum protein is abnormal
  \item HLA-B*15:02 screening is recommended in at-risk Asian populations before initiation \cite{chung2004}, and CYP3A4 inhibitors (macrolides, azole antifungals, verapamil) that raise carbamazepine levels require dose adjustment
  \item Monitor CBC, serum sodium, and liver function tests for blood dyscrasias, hyponatremia, and hepatotoxicity
\end{itemize}
\section*{Risks}
Minimal risk. Slight pain or bruising at the venipuncture site. Rarely: hematoma, infection, or vasovagal syncope.
\section*{What Happens After the Test}
The carbamazepine level is interpreted against the therapeutic range (total 4--12 mcg/mL) in the context of the sampling time and the patient's clinical state. Subtherapeutic troughs warrant a dose increase (often needed in the first weeks of therapy because of autoinduction), while supratherapeutic levels require dose reduction and evaluation for toxicity. A repeat trough level is usually checked 1--2 weeks after any dose adjustment.
\section*{Understanding Results}
Subtherapeutic (<4 mcg/mL): Inadequate seizure control or bipolar mood stabilization. Autoinduction has lowered levels. Toxic (>12 mcg/mL): Nystagmus, ataxia, diplopia, dizziness, sedation. Severe toxicity (>20 mcg/mL): Coma, seizures, respiratory depression, cardiac conduction abnormalities.
\section*{Normal Results}
Therapeutic range: 4--12 mcg/mL (total). Free carbamazepine: 1--4 mcg/mL. Therapeutic range for trigeminal neuralgia: 4--8 mcg/mL may be adequate. Trough level: immediately before next dose (steady state achieved after 3--5 weeks due to autoinduction).
\section*{Follow-up Tests}
Complete blood count (before therapy and periodically for agranulocytosis/aplastic anemia), serum sodium (hyponatremia), liver function tests, HLA-B*15:02 screening in at-risk Asian populations (Han Chinese, Thai, Malaysian, South Asian descent).

\section*{Regulatory Status and Authorization Date}
Regulatory status applies to the specific assay, instrument, manufacturer, and intended use rather than to the test name alone. No single approval date can be assigned to this test category. For a commercial assay, verify the current product in the relevant regulator's database: the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) clearance, approval, or authorization; India's Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) licence or permission; the European Union In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation (EU IVDR) CE certificate issued through the applicable conformity-assessment route; or the United Kingdom Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) registration and UKCA/CE status. Laboratory-developed tests may not have an individual FDA, CDSCO, EU IVDR, or MHRA approval date.
