\chapter{Serum Tryptase}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Serum tryptase measures mast-cell tryptase released into the blood during mast-cell activation. It is most useful as a paired test: an acute sample obtained soon after a suspected anaphylactic reaction is compared with a later baseline sample. Baseline tryptase can also support evaluation for systemic mastocytosis or hereditary alpha-tryptasemia, but it is not diagnostic by itself.

\section*{Alternative Names}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Total tryptase
  \item mast-cell tryptase
  \item acute tryptase
  \item baseline tryptase
\end{itemize}
\section*{Principle}
An immunoassay measures total circulating alpha- and beta-tryptase. The laboratory reports a concentration in ng/mL; assay-specific reference limits and interpretation criteria apply.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Support the diagnosis of anaphylaxis after an unexplained or severe systemic reaction
  \item Investigate recurrent idiopathic anaphylaxis or severe reactions to insect stings, medications, or anesthesia
  \item Evaluate persistently elevated baseline tryptase for systemic mastocytosis, other mast-cell disorders, or hereditary alpha-tryptasemia
  \item Help assess the risk of severe future reactions in selected patients with suspected mast-cell disease
\end{itemize}

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
Venous blood is collected in a serum tube. For suspected anaphylaxis, an acute sample is ideally collected 30 minutes to 2 hours after symptom onset, with a later baseline sample collected at least 24 hours after complete recovery (often during follow-up).

\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
No fasting is required. Record the time of symptom onset, treatment, and blood collection because timing strongly affects interpretation.

\section*{Risks}
Venipuncture only.

\section*{Understanding Results}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Significant acute rise:} A rise from baseline supports systemic mast-cell activation, but a normal result does not exclude anaphylaxis, especially when sampling is delayed or the reaction is mild.
  \item \textbf{Persistently elevated baseline:} May occur with systemic mastocytosis, hereditary alpha-tryptasemia, chronic kidney disease, or other conditions; specialist evaluation and, when indicated, KIT mutation testing or additional hematologic assessment may be needed.
  \item \textbf{Normal baseline:} Does not exclude anaphylaxis or other mast-cell activation disorders.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Follow-up Tests}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Complete blood count, liver and kidney function tests
  \item KIT D816V mutation testing in blood when systemic mastocytosis is suspected
  \item Allergy assessment for an identified trigger and evaluation for hereditary alpha-tryptasemia when baseline tryptase remains elevated
\end{itemize}
