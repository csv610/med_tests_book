\chapter{Factor V Leiden Test}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
This blood test looks for an inherited change called Factor V Leiden. The change makes it easier for blood clots to form in veins. The risk is higher when other risks are present, such as pregnancy, estrogen-containing medicines, long periods of immobility, major illness, cancer, or surgery.

\section*{Alternative Names}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Factor V Leiden Mutation Test
  \item F5 c.1601G>A
  \item Factor V R506Q
  \item Activated Protein C Resistance Genetic Test
  \item Thrombophilia Genetic Test
\end{itemize}
\section*{How It Is Performed}
A blood sample is collected in a tube used for genetic testing. The laboratory looks for the Factor V Leiden change. Some laboratories first use a clotting screen called an activated protein C resistance test. The genetic test can confirm the finding and is less affected by pregnancy or blood-thinning medicines.

\section*{Why It Is Ordered}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Selected evaluation of unexplained or recurrent venous thromboembolism
  \item Assessment of a strong or unusual family history of venous thrombosis
  \item Clarification of an abnormal activated-protein-C-resistance result
  \item Thrombophilia assessment when the result would change counseling or management
\end{itemize}

\section*{Interpretation and Limitations}
A result can show one changed copy of the gene or two changed copies. A positive result means higher risk; it does not prove that the change caused a past clot and cannot predict when a clot will occur. A negative result rules out the tested change, but not every other cause of clotting. The clinician interprets the result together with personal and family history.
\section*{Specimen and Preparation}
Fasting is not needed. Tell the clinician if you are pregnant, use estrogen-containing medicines or blood thinners, recently received a transfusion, or have had a clot. These details may affect a clotting screen. Genetic counseling can help explain what the result may mean for family members.

\section*{Risks and Safety}
The blood draw may cause brief pain or bruising. A positive result does not automatically mean lifelong blood-thinning treatment. Chest pain, sudden breathlessness, coughing blood, or one-sided leg swelling needs urgent medical attention, whether the test is positive or negative.

\section*{Understanding Results and Follow-up}
A negative result means that this particular change was not found. Follow-up may include reviewing your history, discussing testing for some adult relatives, or looking for other causes of clotting. Do not start or stop blood thinners or hormone medicines because of this result alone.
