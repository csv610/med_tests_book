\chapter{Fecal Fat Test}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Fecal-fat testing measures unabsorbed fat in stool and helps evaluate fat malabsorption (steatorrhea). Excess stool fat can result from pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, coeliac disease, other small-intestinal mucosal disease, bile-acid deficiency, or shortened intestinal transit and surface area.

\section*{Alternative Names}
Stool Fat Test; Faecal Fat; Quantitative Fecal Fat; Sudan Stain for Stool Fat; Steatorrhea Test

\section*{How It Is Performed}
A qualitative test may use Sudan staining of a random stool specimen to identify fat globules. A quantitative test usually requires collection of stool over a defined period, commonly 72 hours, while the patient consumes a laboratory-specified amount of dietary fat. The laboratory measures total fecal fat and may calculate the coefficient of fat absorption.

\section*{Why It Is Ordered}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Investigation of chronic diarrhea, bulky or oily stools, weight loss, and suspected steatorrhea
  \item Evaluation of suspected pancreatic exocrine insufficiency or small-bowel malabsorption
  \item Assessment of persistent nutritional deficiencies when routine evaluation is inconclusive
\end{itemize}

\section*{Preparation, Risks, and Limitations}
There is no procedure-related risk, but timed stool collection can be inconvenient. Follow the laboratory's diet and collection instructions exactly. Incomplete collection, inadequate dietary fat intake, contamination with urine or water, and some medications can make the result unreliable. Fecal elastase is often more convenient for assessing pancreatic exocrine insufficiency, while coeliac serology and other targeted tests are needed to identify the underlying cause.
\section*{Specimen, Preparation, and Safety}
Follow the laboratory's collection kit and dietary instructions exactly; quantitative testing commonly requires timed stool collection while consuming the prescribed fat intake. Keep urine, water, and cleaning products out of the container. There is no procedure-related risk, but incomplete collection can invalidate the result.

\section*{Understanding Results and Follow-up}
The report may give qualitative fat, grams of fat per day, or a coefficient of fat absorption. A high result supports fat malabsorption but does not identify the cause. Follow-up may include fecal elastase, coeliac serology, liver tests, nutritional assessment, or other targeted testing; results should be interpreted with symptoms, diet, and collection completeness.
