\chapter{ABO/Rh Blood Typing and Crossmatch}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
ABO/Rh typing identifies a person's ABO blood group and RhD status. Antibody screening looks for clinically significant red-cell antibodies, and a crossmatch checks compatibility between the patient's plasma and donor red cells before transfusion.

\section*{Alternative Names}
Blood Group; Blood Type; ABO Typing; Rh Typing; Type and Screen; Compatibility Testing; Crossmatch.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
It is required before most red-cell transfusions and is used in pregnancy care, blood donation, transfusion-reaction investigation, and preparation for situations in which transfusion may be needed. Antibody identification is particularly important when the screen is positive or the patient has been previously transfused or pregnant.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
A labeled blood sample is tested with antisera to determine A, B, and D antigens. The plasma is screened against reagent red cells for unexpected antibodies. If transfusion is planned, the laboratory selects compatible donor units and performs an electronic, immediate-spin, or antiglobulin crossmatch according to the patient's antibody history and local policy.

\section*{Preparation, Risks, and Limitations}
No fasting is needed. The main safety requirement is correct patient identification and specimen labeling. Risks are limited to blood collection. Recent transfusion, pregnancy, autoimmune antibodies, or an inadequate sample can complicate interpretation and may require additional testing.

\section*{Understanding Results}
The report states ABO group, RhD status, antibody-screen result, and—when performed—compatible donor units. A positive antibody screen requires antibody identification and selection of antigen-negative blood when clinically significant. Emergency transfusion decisions may use uncrossmatched blood when waiting would create greater danger.

\section*{References}
American Association of Blood Banks standards for pretransfusion testing.

\section*{Regulatory Status and Authorization Date}
Regulatory status applies to the specific assay, instrument, manufacturer, and intended use rather than to the test name alone. No single approval date can be assigned to this test category. For a commercial assay, verify the current product in the relevant regulator's database: the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) clearance, approval, or authorization; India's Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) licence or permission; the European Union In Vitro Diagnostic Regulation (EU IVDR) CE certificate issued through the applicable conformity-assessment route; or the United Kingdom Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA) registration and UKCA/CE status. Laboratory-developed tests may not have an individual FDA, CDSCO, EU IVDR, or MHRA approval date.
