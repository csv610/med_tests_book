\chapter{ABO/Rh Blood Typing and Crossmatch}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
ABO/Rh typing identifies a person's ABO blood group and RhD status. Antibody screening looks for clinically significant red-cell antibodies. A crossmatch checks compatibility between the patient's plasma and donor red cells before transfusion.

\section*{Alternative Names}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Blood Group
  \item Blood Type
  \item ABO Typing
  \item Rh Typing
  \item Type and Screen
  \item Compatibility Testing
  \item Crossmatch
\end{itemize}
\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
This test is required before most red-cell transfusions. It is also used in pregnancy care, blood donation, transfusion-reaction investigation, and preparation for situations in which transfusion may be needed. Antibody identification is particularly important when the screen is positive or when the patient has been transfused or pregnant before.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
A labeled blood sample is tested with antisera to determine A, B, and D antigens. The plasma is screened against reagent red cells for unexpected antibodies. If transfusion is planned, the laboratory selects compatible donor units. It then performs an electronic, immediate-spin, or antiglobulin crossmatch according to the patient's antibody history and local policy.

\section*{Preparation, Risks, and Limitations}
No fasting is needed. The main safety requirement is correct patient identification and specimen labeling. Risks are limited to blood collection. Recent transfusion, pregnancy, autoimmune antibodies, or an inadequate sample can complicate interpretation and may require additional testing.

\section*{Understanding Results}
The report states the ABO group, RhD status, antibody-screen result, and, when performed, the compatible donor units. A positive antibody screen requires antibody identification and selection of antigen-negative blood when clinically significant. In an emergency, clinicians may use uncrossmatched blood when waiting would create greater danger.
