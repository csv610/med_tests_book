\chapter{Autism Spectrum Disorder (ASD) Screening}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Autism spectrum disorder screening is a developmental assessment for signs of differences in social communication, interaction, restricted interests, or repetitive behavior. It is not a blood test and cannot by itself diagnose autism.

\section*{Alternative Names}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Autism Screening
  \item ASD Screening
  \item Developmental Screening for Autism
  \item M-CHAT-R/F
\end{itemize}

\section*{Principle}
Screening tools use caregiver responses and clinician observation to identify children who should receive a diagnostic evaluation. The Modified Checklist for Autism in Toddlers, Revised with Follow-Up (M-CHAT-R/F) is commonly used in toddlers, while developmental surveillance continues at every well-child visit.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
Screening is considered when developmental milestones are delayed, communication or social interaction differs from expectations, repetitive behaviors are observed, or caregivers and clinicians have concerns.

\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test}
Screening is a preliminary step. A diagnostic evaluation by appropriately trained professionals considers development, language, cognition, adaptive function, behavior, hearing, and medical history.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
Caregivers complete a questionnaire and the clinician reviews development and observes behavior. A positive screen leads to referral; it does not establish autism, and a negative screen does not rule it out when concerns persist.

\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
No physical preparation is needed. Bring developmental records, school or therapy reports, and examples of behavior across home, childcare, and school settings.

\section*{Understanding Results}
Results indicate whether further assessment is recommended. Developmental level, language, hearing, culture, and the setting in which behavior is observed can affect interpretation.

\section*{Follow-up Tests}
Follow-up may include formal autism diagnostic assessment, hearing evaluation, speech-language evaluation, cognitive and adaptive testing, and genetic or other medical evaluation when indicated by the clinical history.
