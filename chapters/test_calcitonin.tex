\chapter{Calcitonin}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Calcitonin is a 32-amino-acid peptide hormone produced by the parafollicular C cells (calcitonin-secreting cells) of the thyroid gland. These cells originate from the neural crest and constitute approximately 0.1\% of thyroid mass, located in the upper and middle thirds of the thyroid lobes. Calcitonin's primary physiological role is calcium homeostasis: it inhibits osteoclast-mediated bone resorption, promotes renal calcium excretion, and lowers serum calcium levels. However, in humans its physiological significance is limited compared to other calcium-regulating hormones (parathyroid hormone, vitamin D) --- calcitonin knockout mice and humans after total thyroidectomy do not develop hypercalcemia. Clinically, the calcitonin serum test is critical because it serves as the most sensitive and specific tumor marker for medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC), a malignancy of the parafollicular C cells that accounts for 3--5\% of all thyroid cancers. Approximately 75\% of MTC is sporadic, while 25\% is hereditary as part of multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (MEN2A, MEN2B) or familial medullary thyroid carcinoma (FMTC), all caused by germline activating mutations in the RET proto-oncogene. Calcitonin measurement is essential for diagnosis, pre-operative staging (calcitonin correlates with tumor volume and predicts lymph node metastasis), post-operative surveillance (should become undetectable after curative resection), and screening of at-risk family members. Calcitonin doubling time (<6 months, 6--24 months, >24 months) is the most powerful prognostic indicator in MTC, surpassing TNM staging, with a doubling time <6 months associated with a 5-year survival of 25\% compared to >90\% for doubling time >2 years \cite{barbet2005}.

\section*{Alternative Names}
hCT; Human Calcitonin; Thyrocalcitonin; Serum Calcitonin; Calcitonin Tumor Marker; CT; Calcitonin by Immunoassay; MTC Tumor Marker

\section*{Principle}
Calcitonin is measured using a two-site sandwich immunometric assay (chemiluminescent immunoassay). Two monoclonal antibodies targeting different epitopes on the calcitonin molecule (N-terminal and C-terminal, or mid-region and C-terminal) are used: one is a capture antibody bound to a solid phase, and the second is a detection antibody labeled with a chemiluminescent tag (ruthenium on Roche ECLIA, acridinium ester on Abbott Architect, or alkaline phosphatase on Siemens IMMULITE). After incubation and washing, the chemiluminescent signal is proportional to calcitonin concentration. The assay detects monomeric calcitonin (1-32). Functional sensitivity is typically <2 pg/mL for high-sensitivity assays. Pre-analytical considerations are crucial: calcitonin is unstable at room temperature --- specimens must be collected on ice, centrifuged at 4 degrees C within 1 hour, and stored frozen at -20 degrees C or colder. Specimen collection in the fasting state is essential. Proteolytic degradation begins within 1--2 hours at room temperature and can reduce measured calcitonin by 30--50\%. Calcitonin exists in several isoforms in plasma: monomeric, dimeric, and polymeric forms, as well as procalcitonin. The assay must specifically detect monomeric calcitonin with minimal cross-reactivity to other isoforms. Stimulated calcitonin testing using a calcium infusion (calcium gluconate 2.5 mg/kg over 30 seconds) or pentagastrin (0.5 mcg/kg over 10 seconds, where available) increases diagnostic sensitivity for MTC. Calcium-stimulated calcitonin >100 pg/mL is considered diagnostic for MTC.

\section*{History}
Calcitonin was discovered in 1962 by Douglas Copp and colleagues, who initially believed it originated from the parathyroid glands and named it "calcitonin" \cite{copp1962}. Later in the same year, Iain MacIntyre and colleagues at Hammersmith Hospital demonstrated that calcitonin originated from thyroid parafollicular C cells. The 32-amino-acid sequence of human calcitonin was determined by Neher and colleagues in 1968. In 1970, Tashjian and Melvin demonstrated that medullary thyroid carcinoma cells produce excessive amounts of calcitonin, establishing calcitonin as the first tumor marker with single-cell-type specificity. The first radioimmunoassay for serum calcitonin was developed by Deftos and colleagues in 1971. The role of RET mutations in MEN2 was discovered by Donis-Keller and colleagues in 1993, leading to the era of prophylactic thyroidectomy based on genetic testing. Calcitonin stimulated testing with pentagastrin was developed in the 1980s and with calcium in the 1970s. The 2015 ATA MTC guidelines established calcitonin doubling time as the major prognostic factor \cite{wells2015}, and the 2016 NCCN and ESMO guidelines incorporated calcitonin measurement into standardized management algorithms. The development of high-sensitivity calcitonin assays (<1 pg/mL sensitivity) in the 2000s has improved early detection of C-cell hyperplasia and micro-MTC.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pre-operative diagnosis of medullary thyroid carcinoma in patients with thyroid nodules --- elevated basal calcitonin >10--30 pg/mL in a patient with thyroid nodule has a positive predictive value of 25--50\% for MTC; stimulated calcitonin >100 pg/mL has PPV >90\%
  \item Screening for medullary thyroid carcinoma in at-risk family members with known RET germline mutations (MEN2A, MEN2B, FMTC) --- annual monitoring from childhood, with age of onset depending on specific RET codon mutation (ATA risk categories: HST, H, MOD)
  \item Post-operative surveillance for MTC recurrence after total thyroidectomy --- calcitonin should fall to undetectable (<2 pg/mL) after complete curative resection; detectable or rising calcitonin indicates residual or recurrent disease
  \item Pre-operative staging of MTC --- basal calcitonin levels correlate with tumor size and lymph node metastasis risk: calcitonin <20 pg/mL predicts node-negative disease in >90\%; 50--200 pg/mL predicts ipsilateral central/lateral node metastases; >200 pg/mL predicts contralateral node metastases; >500 pg/mL predicts distant metastases
  \item Monitoring of unresectable or metastatic MTC disease progression --- calcitonin doubling time is the single most important prognostic factor
  \item Diagnostic workup of thyroid nodule when FNA cytology is suggestive but not diagnostic of MTC (atypia of undetermined significance with MTC features, or suspicious for MTC)
  \item Universal calcitonin screening in all patients with thyroid nodules (routine in European guidelines, controversial in US guidelines --- ETA and ESMO recommend screening, ATA does not due to high false-positive rate from C-cell hyperplasia)
  \item Monitoring response to targeted therapy (vandetanib, cabozantinib, selpercatinib, pralsetinib) in advanced MTC --- reduced calcitonin correlates with tumor response
\end{itemize}

\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test}
This is both a primary screening test for medullary thyroid carcinoma in thyroid nodule evaluation (recommended by European Thyroid Association and ESMO guidelines) and a tumor marker for post-operative surveillance. Whether basal calcitonin should be measured in all patients with thyroid nodules remains controversial: European guidelines (ETA 2006, ESMO 2013) and Korean guidelines recommend universal calcitonin measurement, while the 2015 ATA MTC guidelines state that routine calcitonin measurement cannot be recommended for or against in thyroid nodules. In practice, many US centers perform selective calcitonin screening based on suspicious ultrasound features, positive family history, or suspicious FNA cytology. In patients with known MTC, calcitonin is the primary biochemical marker for diagnosis, staging, and surveillance of disease.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
A healthcare professional draws a fasting blood sample from a vein in your arm. The specimen must be handled with special care due to calcitonin's instability: blood is collected into a chilled plain red-top tube or serum separator tube, placed on ice immediately, transported to the laboratory within 30 minutes, centrifuged at 4 degrees C within 1 hour, and the serum is aliquoted and stored frozen at -20 degrees C or colder. If the specimen is not kept cold, calcitonin degrades within 1--2 hours at room temperature, resulting in falsely low results of 30--50\% below the true value. For stimulated calcitonin testing, the patient receives an intravenous infusion of calcium gluconate (2.5 mg/kg elemental calcium over 30 seconds), and blood samples are drawn at baseline and 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, and 15 minutes after injection. Alternatively, pentagastrin stimulation (0.5 mcg/kg IV over 10 seconds) may be used where pentagastrin is available (not available in the United States since 2011; calcium stimulation is the alternative).

\section*{What Preparation Is Needed}
Fasting for 10--12 hours is essential before calcitonin testing because food intake (especially high-calcium meals) can physiologically stimulate calcitonin release. Alcohol consumption should be avoided for 24 hours before testing. Patients should be seated for 15--30 minutes before venipuncture. The specimen requires immediate cold processing as described above. No biotin supplements should be taken for 48 hours before testing (interferes with streptavidin-biotin immunoassay platforms). For stimulated testing, adequate hydration and pre-test evaluation of renal function are required because the calcium load can produce nausea, flushing, and transient blood pressure changes. Patients on proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole, pantoprazole) and H2-receptor antagonists (famotidine, ranitidine) may have elevated basal calcitonin due to hypergastrinemia-induced C-cell stimulation --- these medications may need to be held for 2 weeks before testing if clinically feasible, though interpretation guidelines vary.

\section*{Contraindications and Precautions}
Important factors that elevate calcitonin and produce false-positive results (calcitonin <100 pg/mL without MTC): (1) gender --- calcitonin values are higher in males than females (male upper limit approximately 10--18 pg/mL vs. female 5--10 pg/mL depending on assay); (2) age --- calcitonin is higher in infants and young children (age-specific reference ranges required for pediatric RET mutation carriers); (3) C-cell hyperplasia --- physiologic (neonates, elderly) or pathologic reactive hyperplasia (hyperparathyroidism, chronic lymphocytic thyroiditis/Hashimoto, hypergastrinemia) can elevate calcitonin without MTC; (4) hypercalcemia --- calcium acutely stimulates calcitonin release; (5) hypergastrinemia --- proton pump inhibitors, autoimmune atrophic gastritis with achlorhydria, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (gastrinoma) stimulating C-cells; (6) chronic renal failure --- decreased renal clearance of calcitonin, levels may be 2--10 fold elevated without MTC; (7) neuroendocrine tumors --- small cell lung cancer, bronchial carcinoid, pancreatic neuroendocrine tumors can ectopically produce calcitonin (<5\% of cases); (8) autoimmune thyroiditis --- lymphocytic infiltration with reactive C-cell hyperplasia; (9) heterophile antibodies and human anti-mouse antibodies (HAMA) --- can cause falsely elevated results in sandwich immunoassays; (10) procalcitonin elevation in sepsis --- procalcitonin may cross-react in some calcitonin assays. Pre-analytical: specimens incorrectly handled (not kept cold, delayed processing) yield falsely low results. Stimulated calcium testing contraindications: known coronary artery disease, cardiac arrhythmias, severe hypertension, pregnancy.

\section*{Risks}
Minimal risk from venipuncture (slight pain, bruising, hematoma). Rare: vasovagal syncope or infection. Calcium stimulation test risks: transient nausea, flushing (30--60 seconds after injection, subsides within 2 minutes), metallic taste, headache, mild blood pressure decrease, bradycardia (reflex vagal response), and very rarely cardiac arrhythmia. Pentagastrin stimulation (where available): nausea, substernal/burning abdominal discomfort, flushing, transient tachycardia.

\section*{What Happens After the Test}
Results are available within 1--3 days (calcitonin testing is often a send-out test to reference laboratories due to specimen handling requirements and lower testing volumes). For thyroid nodule evaluation: basal calcitonin <8--10 pg/mL (female) or <15--18 pg/mL (male) effectively excludes MTC and no further workup is needed. Basal calcitonin 10--100 pg/mL is indeterminate --- stimulated testing (calcium or pentagastrin) is recommended; if stimulated calcitonin >100 pg/mL, MTC is highly likely and total thyroidectomy with central neck dissection is indicated, regardless of FNA results. Calcitonin >100 pg/mL basal is virtually diagnostic of MTC (or extensive C-cell hyperplasia) and surgery is recommended. Post-operatively: calcitonin should be measured at 1--3 months after surgery; undetectable calcitonin indicates biochemical cure. Detectable calcitonin after surgery indicates residual disease; calcitonin doubling time is calculated from serial measurements (at least 3 measurements over minimum 2 years for accuracy). Median calcitonin doubling time categories: <6 months (most aggressive, 5-year survival 25\%), 6--24 months (intermediate), >24 months (indolent, 5-year survival >90\%).

\section*{Understanding Results}

\subsection*{Below Normal}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Undetectable/low calcitonin (<2 pg/mL) post-total thyroidectomy for MTC:} Biochemical cure. Risk of structural recurrence is <5\% at 10 years. Annual calcitonin and CEA monitoring recommended.
  \item \textbf{Negative basal calcitonin (<8 pg/mL female, <15 pg/mL male) in thyroid nodule evaluation:} Excludes clinically significant MTC with high negative predictive value (>99\%). No further MTC workup required.
  \item \textbf{Negative stimulated calcitonin (peak <100 pg/mL after calcium injection):} Excludes MTC. C-cell hyperplasia may still be present but management is different from MTC (no surgery required unless there is strong family history or RET mutation).
  \item \textbf{Declining calcitonin doubling time >2 years after incomplete resection or in metastatic MTC:} Favorable prognosis with indolent disease course. Active surveillance may be appropriate over aggressive intervention.
\end{itemize}

\subsection*{Above Normal}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin >100 pg/mL in a patient with thyroid nodule:} Diagnostic of medullary thyroid carcinoma. Total thyroidectomy with prophylactic central (level VI) neck dissection is indicated. Pre-operative staging should include calcitonin/CEA, neck ultrasound, and if calcitonin >500 pg/mL, CT chest/abdomen and bone scan for distant metastasis.
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin 30--100 pg/mL:} Strongly suspicious for MTC (PPV 25--50\%). Calcium-stimulated calcitonin should be obtained. If stimulated calcitonin >100 pg/mL, MTC surgery is recommended. If stimulated calcitonin 30--100 pg/mL, C-cell hyperplasia or micro-MTC (<1 cm) is likely.
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin 10--30 pg/mL:} Gray zone. May represent physiologic elevation, C-cell hyperplasia, or early micro-MTC. Gender-specific interpretation: male upper limit typically 15--18 pg/mL, female upper limit 5--10 pg/mL. Stimulated testing recommended to clarify. If stimulated calcitonin is <30 pg/mL, C-cell hyperplasia is more likely than MTC and observation with annual calcitonin may be appropriate.
  \item \textbf{Post-operative detectable or rising calcitonin after MTC resection:} Indicates residual or recurrent disease. The calcitonin doubling time should be calculated. If calcitonin is low and stable and doubling time is >2 years, the prognosis is excellent with observation. If doubling time is <6 months, targeted systemic therapy (vandetanib, cabozantinib, or selpercatinib for RET-mutant MTC) should be considered because disease progression is rapid.
  \item \textbf{Hypercalcitoninemia from non-MTC causes:} Chronic kidney disease (2--10 fold elevation), proton pump inhibitor therapy (block gastric acid, cause hypergastrinemia, stimulate C-cells), autoimmune atrophic gastritis with pernicious anemia, Zollinger-Ellison syndrome (gastrinoma), neuroendocrine tumors with ectopic calcitonin production, Hashimoto thyroiditis with C-cell hyperplasia, hypercalcemia from primary hyperparathyroidism, neonates and infants (physiologic elevation), cigarette smoking (mild elevation), and alcohol consumption.
  \item \textbf{RET mutation carriers with elevated calcitonin:} By definition indicates C-cell hyperplasia or MTC has developed and timing of prophylactic thyroidectomy should be determined based on ATA risk category (HST: thyroidectomy before 1 year for MEN2B with RET M918T mutation; H: thyroidectomy at or before age 5 for RET codon 634/883 mutations; MOD: thyroidectomy when calcitonin becomes elevated or if family prefers, for codon 609/611/618/620/630/768/790/791/804/891 mutations).
\end{itemize}

\section*{Normal Results}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin (Female):} <5--10 pg/mL (varies by assay; Roche ECLIA: <5 pg/mL, Siemens IMMULITE: <11.5 pg/mL)
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin (Male):} <10--18 pg/mL (consistently higher than females; Roche ECLIA: <12 pg/mL, Siemens IMMULITE: <18 pg/mL)
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin (Children <5 years):} <15 pg/mL (age-specific reference ranges required)
  \item \textbf{Basal calcitonin (Children 5--15 years):} <10--15 pg/mL
  \item \textbf{Stimulated calcitonin (calcium infusion, peak value):} <30 pg/mL (normal); 30--100 pg/mL (indeterminate, possible C-cell hyperplasia); >100 pg/mL (diagnostic for MTC)
  \item \textbf{Stimulated calcitonin (pentagastrin, peak value):} <30 pg/mL female, <80 pg/mL male (normal); >100 pg/mL (diagnostic for MTC)
  \item \textbf{Post-thyroidectomy for MTC (target):} <2 pg/mL (undetectable = biochemical cure)
  \item \textbf{Note:} Calcitonin assays are not standardized across manufacturers, and there is significant inter-assay variability. Always use the same assay for serial monitoring. The reference range must account for gender and assay-specific values.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Follow-up Tests}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{RET proto-oncogene genetic analysis:} Mandatory in all patients with newly diagnosed MTC. Mutation analysis of exons 5, 8, 10, 11, 13--16. Germline RET mutations identify hereditary MTC (MEN2A, MEN2B, FMTC) in approximately 25\% of patients, with implications for family screening and prophylactic thyroidectomy. Somatic RET mutations (most commonly M918T in exons 15/16) are found in 40--50\% of sporadic MTC and predict response to RET-selective tyrosine kinase inhibitors (selpercatinib, pralsetinib).
  \item \textbf{Carcinoembryonic antigen (CEA):} Co-measured with calcitonin in MTC management. CEA has a longer half-life (3--5 days) and provides complementary information. Rising CEA with stable calcitonin suggests dedifferentiation or more aggressive disease. Discrepancy between calcitonin and CEA is an adverse prognostic sign.
  \item \textbf{Calcium-stimulated calcitonin testing:} Performed when basal calcitonin is in the indeterminate range (10--100 pg/mL) to distinguish benign C-cell hyperplasia from MTC. Calcium gluconate 2.5 mg/kg IV over 30 seconds with calcitonin measurements at 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, and 15 minutes.
  \item \textbf{Neck ultrasound with lymph node mapping:} High-resolution imaging to evaluate thyroid mass (irregular margins, microcalcifications, hypoechoic appearance typical of MTC) and cervical lymph node compartments (levels I--VI). MTC frequently metastasizes to lateral compartment nodes early in disease.
  \item \textbf{Fine-needle aspiration (FNA) of thyroid nodule with calcitonin immunocytochemistry:} If MTC is suspected on ultrasound or calcitonin is elevated. Calcitonin immunostaining confirms MTC. FNA calcitonin washout concentration >36 pg/mL has been used for lymph node confirmation.
  \item \textbf{CT neck, chest, abdomen with contrast:} For staging when calcitonin >500 pg/mL, or for recurrence workup when calcitonin is rising. Evaluates for cervical, mediastinal, pulmonary, hepatic, and adrenal metastases.
  \item \textbf{Bone scintigraphy or FDG-PET/CT:} For suspected bone metastases (calcitonin often >1000 pg/mL with skeletal involvement). FDG-PET/CT is preferred for detecting distant metastases in aggressive MTC with short calcitonin doubling time.
  \item \textbf{Ga-68 DOTATATE or F-18 DOPA PET/CT:} Somatostatin receptor-based imaging or DOPA PET for detecting residual/recurrent MTC when standard imaging is negative but calcitonin is elevated.
  \item \textbf{24-hour urinary metanephrines and plasma free metanephrines:} Excludes pheochromocytoma before thyroidectomy in patients with MEN2. Pheochromocytoma should be resected before thyroid surgery to prevent hypertensive crisis during anesthesia.
  \item \textbf{Serum calcium and PTH:} Before thyroidectomy to exclude concurrent primary hyperparathyroidism (in MEN2A, hyperparathyroidism develops in 10--30\%). If present, subtotal parathyroidectomy or total parathyroidectomy with autotransplantation is performed at the time of thyroidectomy.
\end{itemize}
