\chapter{Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy}
\section*{What Is This Test?} Sentinel lymph node biopsy removes the first lymph node or nodes draining a tumor to assess possible spread.\section*{Alternative Names} Sentinel node biopsy; SLNB; lymphatic mapping.\section*{Principle} Dye, radioactive tracer, or both identify the likely first draining node, which is examined by pathology.\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered} It stages selected breast cancers, melanomas, and other cancers when nodal spread is possible but not clinically apparent.\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test} It is a staging procedure after cancer diagnosis; imaging and clinical examination guide selection.\section*{How This Test Is Performed} Tracer is injected near the tumor, the node is located during surgery, removed, and examined microscopically.\section*{What Preparation Is Needed} Follow surgical fasting and medicine instructions; report allergies, pregnancy, and lymphedema risk.\section*{Understanding Results} Negative nodes lower the likelihood of regional spread; isolated tumor cells, micrometastasis, or macrometastasis affect staging and treatment.\section*{Follow-up Tests} Additional node surgery, imaging, molecular pathology, or oncology consultation may follow.\section*{References} \begin{enumerate}\item MedlinePlus.
