\chapter{Beta-D-Glucan}

\section*{What Is This Test?}
Beta-D-glucan is a cell-wall component released by many fungi. Serum beta-D-glucan testing is a supportive biomarker for selected invasive fungal infections, including invasive candidiasis, aspergillosis, and Pneumocystis jirovecii pneumonia.

\section*{Alternative Names}
BDG; 1,3-Beta-D-Glucan; Fungal Biomarker Test.

\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered}
Testing is used in selected high-risk patients with possible invasive fungal infection, such as those with prolonged neutropenia, hematologic malignancy, transplant, intensive-care illness, or unexplained pneumonia. It is intended to complement—not replace—culture, antigen, molecular, and clinical assessment.

\section*{How This Test Is Performed}
A blood sample is collected into a specialized tube and analyzed by a sensitive colorimetric or kinetic assay. Serial measurements may be more informative than a single result in high-risk patients.

\section*{Preparation, Risks, and Limitations}
No fasting is required. Venipuncture is the usual risk. Hemodialysis, albumin or immunoglobulin products, some antibiotics, gauze exposure, severe mucositis, and sample contamination can cause false-positive results. The test does not reliably detect Mucorales or Cryptococcus and does not identify the organism.

\section*{Understanding Results}
A positive result increases suspicion but is not diagnostic by itself. A negative result lowers the likelihood of some invasive fungal infections but cannot exclude disease, particularly after antifungal treatment or with organisms poorly detected by the assay.

\section*{References}
European Organisation for Research and Treatment of Cancer and Mycoses Study Group guidance on invasive fungal disease definitions.
