\chapter{PD-L1 (Immunotherapy) Tests}
\section*{What Is This Test?} PD-L1 testing measures programmed death-ligand 1 expression in tumor tissue to help determine eligibility for selected immunotherapies.\section*{Alternative Names} PD-L1 IHC; programmed death-ligand 1 test; immune checkpoint biomarker.\section*{Principle} Immunohistochemistry detects PD-L1 staining and reports a scoring system such as tumor proportion score or combined positive score.\section*{Why This Test Is Ordered} It supports treatment decisions in selected lung, breast, gastric, esophageal, cervical, urothelial, and other cancers.\section*{Primary vs Secondary Test} It is a secondary pathology biomarker after tissue diagnosis; the assay and drug indication must match.\section*{How This Test Is Performed} A pathologist stains biopsy or surgical tissue using a validated companion or complementary diagnostic assay.\section*{What Preparation Is Needed} No patient preparation beyond tissue sampling.\section*{Understanding Results} Positive and negative thresholds vary by cancer, specimen, assay, and treatment; a negative result does not exclude all benefit.\section*{Follow-up Tests} HER2, hormone receptors, genomic tests, staging imaging, and oncology consultation may follow.\section*{References} \begin{enumerate}\item MedlinePlus.
